Chiswick High Road

William Michael Wilds grew up in Chiswick, west London. As a teenager he cycled to the showroom of The Chequered Flag, a racing-car dealer on Chiswick High Road, and ended up with a Saturday job washing the cars. Chas Beattie, who built Gemini Formula Junior cars there, took him to Brands Hatch. "That was it for me," he told Motor Sport in 2005. "I knew that my future had to be in motor racing."

At the end of 1964 he borrowed £280, with his mother guaranteeing the loan, and bought a DRW Mk1 to race in the 750 Motor Club's 1172 Formula. His first race, at Snetterton in May 1965, ended in third place. A win at Silverstone followed later that year.

The climb was slow and always short of money. He sold the DRW to pay debts, won Formula 4 races in a Vixen, and in 1971 took a job as a Firestone tyre technician. That put him in the paddock at international meetings and helped find him a Formula Ford drive in a Titan. In 1973 he raced in all three British Formula 3 championships, winning at Croft ahead of Tony Brise and at Mallory Park ahead of Jacques Laffite.

Grand Prix, 1974 to 1976

In 1974 Wilds moved up to Formula 5000 in Dempster's March 74A and finished second at Brands Hatch and Mallory Park. March picked him to stand in for the injured Hans Stuck at the Swedish Grand Prix, but he hurt his wrist in a crash at Thruxton the next day and Reine Wisell took the seat. What followed was eight World Championship entries in three seasons, always in modest machinery.

Watkins Glen · 6 October 1974

First start, and a word from Scheckter

Mo Nunn's Ensign team saved a fresh set of tyres for him, and Wilds made a Grand Prix grid for the first time, sharing it with Ronnie Peterson, "my absolute hero". After qualifying, Ken Tyrrell told him Jody Scheckter wanted a word. Wilds expected to be told off for being slow. Scheckter wanted to praise how he had handled the old Ensign N174.

In the race a fuel-pressure problem cost him a long pit stop. His friend Helmuth Koinigg was killed in the same race.

Stanley-BRM · January 1975

Two races and out

After testing a BRM P160, Wilds drove the P201 in the first two rounds of 1975. The V12 failed in Argentina and the clutch in Brazil. When he criticised the engine and argued the team would be better off with a Ford Cosworth DFV, Louis Stanley dropped him. Bob Evans had the car for South Africa.

In 1976 he tried once more, at Brands Hatch in Team P.R. Reilly's Shadow DN3B, and did not qualify. He looked back plainly: "To be honest I wasn't the best driver in the world."

Le Mans and Ecurie Ecosse

Wilds raced the 24 Hours seven times between 1981 and 1988 and saw the flag once. His best sports-car years came with the Scottish team Ecurie Ecosse in Group C2. He called the C2 class win at the 1985 Silverstone 1000km, shared with Ray Mallock, the greatest achievement of his career. In 1986 Ecosse won the C2 teams' world title, which he lists among his honours.

    First race, in May, at Snetterton in a DRW Mk1: third place. A win at Silverstone follows.

  2. 1966

    Heavy crash at Brands Hatch. Fractured pelvis and broken nose. Tiny Littler repairs the chassis for free.

  3. 1970

    Formula 4 wins in a Vixen, two of them at Mallory Park.

  4. 1971

    Formula Ford in a Titan, with support from his employer, Firestone.

  5. 1973

    First Formula 3 win, at Croft. Wins again at Mallory Park in October, ahead of Jacques Laffite and Brian Henton.

  6. 1974

    Formula 5000 with Dempster's March 74A. First Grand Prix entry at Brands Hatch in July; first Grand Prix start at Watkins Glen in October.

  7. 1975

    Two Grands Prix for Stanley-BRM. Also works as sales director for a friend's helicopter company.

  8. 1976

    Fifth in the ShellSport 5000 European Championship. Fails to qualify for the British Grand Prix.

  9. 1978

    Wins the Formula 2 class of the Aurora AFX British F1 Championship in Graham Eden's Ralt RT1.

  10. 1981

    Le Mans debut, in a Porsche 935.

  11. 1983

    Pole and victory in a Williams FW07 on 1 April, in the only round the British F1 series ran that year. Later, at Donington, his Lola T280 catches fire and he spends two weeks in hospital with burns.

  12. 1984

    Thoroughbred Sports Cars champion in an Aston Martin DB4. Joins Ecurie Ecosse in Group C2.

  13. 1985

    C2 class win in the Silverstone 1000km with Ray Mallock.

  14. 1988

    Last Le Mans: 14th in a Nissan R88C.

  15. 1992–98

    Four RJB Mining Historic Sports Car titles, in 1992, 1993, 1996 and 1998.

  16. 1994

    Breaks both legs and both ankles when the ex-Gilles Villeneuve Ferrari 312T3 he is demonstrating crashes at the Goodwood Festival of Speed. Later returns to win at Donington in a Chevron B8.

  17. 2008

    Britcar champion with his son Anthony and Ian Lawson in a BMW. Wins the Group C Enduro Trophy with Henry Pearman in a Porsche 962.

  18. 2013

    Britcar Production Cup champion again, with Anthony and Ian Lawson.

  19. 2016

    Britcar Endurance Class 2 champion with Anthony in a Ferrari 458 GTC.

  20. 2018

    Aged 72, races in the Porsche Carrera Cup GB finale at Brands Hatch with Redline Racing. Voted MsD Pro-Am Instructor of the Year.

  21. 2022

    Racing the ex-Mario Andretti Kenwood Porsche 956 in Peter Auto's Classic Group C series. Publishes his memoir, Life on the Wilds Side!

  22. 2023

    The memoir wins the SAHB Michael Sedgwick Award.

Accidents

When Wilds started, British circuit tickets carried the warning "Motor Racing is dangerous" on the back. He found out several times.

Brands Hatch · 1966

A fractured pelvis and a broken nose in his second season. The review of his memoir describes an early crash at Paddock Hill Bend that left him unconscious for a week.

Thruxton · 1974

A Formula 5000 crash the day after March chose him for the Swedish Grand Prix. The wrist injury cost him the drive.

Donington Park · 1983

The fuel tank of his Lola T280 split during a Thundersports race and the car caught fire. Two weeks in hospital with burns.

Goodwood · 1994

Demonstrating the ex-Gilles Villeneuve Ferrari 312T3 at the Festival of Speed, he crashed and broke both legs and both ankles. His legs nearly had to be amputated.

In his words

Off the track

  • Instructor

    He has taught on track since the early 1970s. He holds a Grade A instructor licence with the Association of Racing Driver Schools and was voted the 2018 MsD Pro-Am Instructor of the Year. He offers driving tuition and after-dinner talks through his own website.

  • Pilot

    He learned to fly both fixed-wing aircraft and helicopters, and went on to instruct on helicopters. In 1975 he was sales director for a friend's helicopter company.

  • Organiser

    He worked as Competitions Manager for the British Racing and Sports Car Club, one of the main clubs that run British circuit racing.

  • Family and home

    He has two sons. Anthony shared his Britcar titles in 2008, 2013 and 2016. Wilds lives on England's south coast and spends his spare time on the water.

The memoir · 2022

Subtitled My Half Century-Plus As A Professional Racing Driver. He wrote it partly so his grandchildren would know what he had done. It won the 2023 Society of Automotive Historians in Britain Michael Sedgwick Award.

Where the sources disagree

  • Wikipedia's text says he raced at Le Mans eight times, but its own table, and F1 Forgotten Drivers, list seven starts. This page uses seven.
  • Wikipedia calls him a 1986 C2 world champion. His own site and instructor profile describe it as the C2 teams' title won with Ecurie Ecosse, which is how it appears here.
  • Some accounts say he retired on lap 50 at Watkins Glen in 1974; Wikipedia's results table records him as not classified, as this page does.
  • His total of British titles is given as 12, 13 or 14 depending on the source and the year it was written.
